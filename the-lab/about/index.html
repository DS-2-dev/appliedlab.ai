About

The Applied AI Lab at Weber State University

A student-led organization that prepares students for the AI frontier and connects students and businesses to solve frontier problems with AI. It includes every college and discipline at Weber State University.

Purpose

To bring people together, ask hard questions, and elevate everyone involved.

  • When a Member leaves the Lab, they leave with the ability to tackle large, ambiguous problems with confidence, to think critically about the world around them, and the candor and consideration to guide others to do the same.
  • When a Partner engages the Lab, they leave with evidence of what students can do with AI in their domain, and the confidence to take risks with AI and newcomers to transform their industry.
  • As Representatives conduct the operation, they transfer their observations into their curriculums, aligning taught skills with industry needs.

Our priorities

To build a model that transforms America's student-employer working relationships.

By building simple, efficient systems that encourage students to attempt work outside their skillsets, we engage and develop essential critical thinking skills supported by a fail-and-try-again foundation. Our hands-on, forward-thinking approach builds connections between students and employers, and it bridges institutional trust between educational institutions and organizations.

Our approach

We use AI to bridge the gap between idea and execution. Everyone has ideas, and few have the skills to execute them. The gap is rarely in an individual's critical thinking or creativity. It is in the hard skills required to execute. So we focus on developing one skill first: AI.

Using AI to supplement hard skills lets students build something they can conceptualize, then work backward into the hard skills and understanding that matter.

Our system enables this by starting with the end in mind. Learning is top down, not bottom up.

What we believe

AI is a means to an end, not the end itself.

We believe AI is re-defining jobs across the global economy, and that AI will be a tool people interact with daily. It will be used to create tools, workflows, and automations that help people do their jobs more efficiently, leaving them to focus on the more rewarding parts of their work. We want to help students and industries get ahead, and capture those rewards early.

AI cannot supplement understanding, only skills.

We believe AI is a tool that can help people reach their highest potential, and that only comes if they commit to being lifelong learners and understand the limits of AI capabilities.

AI won't replace jobs, but the people who use it will.

We do not believe AI on its own can replace the intuition and instincts of people. That will not stop a person from using AI to build an automation that does. To prepare students for this reality, we create an environment that enables students to become multi-dimensional thinkers, capable of understanding complex systems and building interpersonal relationships that cannot be automated.

AI carries the values of the people who build it.

We believe no model is neutral. Some are built with ethics at their core, and others are built to hold your attention because attention is what they sell. We ask who built a system, what it optimizes for, and whether it is honest about its limits.

Culture

Values are cheap when nothing is going wrong, so each value is evidenced by real situations.

Be excellent.

Excellence here means work you can stand behind and explain.

The AI produced something that works, and you do not fully understand it.
We: Take it apart until you can explain why it is right and where a person still has to be.
Avoid: Shipping it and hoping nobody asks. You will be asked, at the showcase.
Your Partner or Representative says the work is not good enough.
We: Ask what specifically is missing, write it down, and come back with a revision.
Avoid: Defending the first version, or reading the note as a verdict on you.

Be kind.

Kindness here is candor with care, especially for whoever just walked in.

A first-time attendee from a non-technical major is sitting alone.
We: Ask what they do every week that takes too long, and work on that with them. Everyone has one.
Avoid: Letting the room split into people who know and people who do not.
Someone's work will not hold up.
We: Say it early, about the work, with the next concrete step.
Avoid: Softening it until it means nothing, or saving it until it is too late.

Work hard.

Hard work here is showing up, saying where you are, and filing the update even when it is late.

You are behind, and you have not filed a weekly update in two weeks.
We: File it late anyway, and say what stalled. A leaky ship beats no ship.
Avoid: Waiting for good news. Silence turns a slow week into a dead project.
The project is harder than you thought and you are not sure you can finish.
We: Say so early, to your Officer or your Representative, and ask for the specific help you need.
Avoid: Hoping it resolves on its own, or handing over work you cannot stand behind.

Standing commitments

These are promises the Lab publishes and keeps.

Ownership of member work.
Members own the solutions they build. Member solutions appear in the Partner's view and at the showcase under the Member's name, and a Partner who wants to use one makes an agreement with the Member.
Everything is show-up.
Meetings, workshops, socials, and the showcase require no registration. Forms collect contact information for reminders and never gate entry.
Funded tools.
A Member with an active project gets a Claude account funded by the Lab. Keeping it requires staying active and maintaining 60% attendance each semester.
Replies.
Inquiries from faculty and organizations get a reply within five business days.

Who runs the Lab

Advisor
A faculty member responsible for enforcing student-leader responsibilities, guiding new leaders to lead the Lab, and participating in and encouraging its future developments. The Advisor is the primary source for transferring institutional knowledge across leadership turnover, and mentors leaders on how to lead the Lab effectively.
President
The face of the Applied AI Lab. The President does not need to be in the GSBE, and may come from any department or college.
Officers
Students who take up various roles and assist the President, and who are the first responders for Members starting their work: onboarding, plan-of-action coaching, and unsticking early work.
Representatives
The liaison connecting Members and Partners, and the quality gate of the Lab's work. They establish and maintain Partner relationships in their domain.
