Students

Any major, any year. Students take on problems from organizations, or ideas of their own, and build working tools with AI.

What it asks of you

  • Coming to meetings. Meetings are open, with no sign-up.

  • Taking on a project. Claim a problem from the Notice Board, alone or with a team, or start one of your own.

  • Owning the plan. Write a thorough action plan (we recommend at least 800 words), then log progress and answer check-ins when milestones come due.

  • Work you can defend. Your submission answers the post’s definition of success with evidence. Your Representative may question any claim, and work you can’t explain or defend is returned.

The levels

An Admin sets each member’s level each semester.

What you get

  • Work with your name on it, reviewed by faculty, which can be published as a story when it’s done.

  • Ownership. What you build is yours, and an organization that wants to use it makes an agreement with you.

  • Teammates and mentors from across the university.

How to start

  1. Come to a meeting.

  2. Join with your Weber State address.

  3. Claim a problem from the Notice Board, or start your own project.

Businesses and organizations

Businesses, nonprofits, local organizations, university offices and clubs bring problems to the Lab. Students build the answers, and a faculty Representative reviews the work at each step.

What it asks of you

  • A thorough post. The problem as it is today, what success looks like, and what students will have to work with. Your Representative can help with the draft, and everything in a post may be processed by AI tools, so share only what your organization allows.

  • Answers to questions. Your Representative and the students on your projects may ask about the problem as they work.

  • Honest feedback on delivered work. If a tool falls short, say what’s missing against the milestones you set.

What you get

  • Work built for your problem, approved by a faculty member before it reaches you.

  • A clear view of the work. Your Pipeline shows each project under your post, who is working on it and how it’s moving.

  • A way to keep going. You can adopt a delivered project and plan a next phase with the students. Students own what they build, so using a tool starts with an agreement with them.

How to start

  1. Create an account for your organization.

  2. Post a problem.

  3. Your Representative reviews the post, and once approved, the post goes live on the Notice Board.

Faculty Representatives

A Representative is a faculty member who guides students’ work for organizations and reviews it at each checkpoint. Any college, department or club at Weber State can have one.

What it asks of you

  • Relationships with organizations in your area. You’re the Lab’s contact for the businesses you cover, and your notes on each one are shared with the other Representatives.

  • Reviews. Posts before they go live, plans before work begins, and finished work before it’s delivered, all in the Approval Hub.

  • Keeping projects moving. Milestones on time, and help for a team that stalls.

What you get

  • A close view of how students use AI on real problems in your field.

  • Observations to bring into your courses, so what’s taught keeps pace with what organizations need.

  • Connections with organizations in your area.

How to start

  1. Request a faculty account with your Weber State address.

  2. An Admin confirms the account.

  3. Your Approval Hub and the organizations you cover appear in your Workspace.
